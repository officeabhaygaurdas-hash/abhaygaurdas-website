1. Executive Summary
Abhay Gaur Prabhuji is presented in the source interview as a monk, preacher, youth mentor, 
administrator, financial leader and donor-care leader serving within ISKCON Delhi. His journey combines 
a devotional upbringing, nearly a decade of Gurukul education in Vrindavan, formal commerce 
education in Delhi, youth outreach, large-scale event organization, institutional finance, donor 
relationship management and temple-project coordination.
According to the interview, he was born in 1994 in Khatauli, Muzaffarnagar district, Uttar Pradesh. He 
joined Bhaktivedanta Gurukula in Vrindavan in 2003 while studying in Class III and continued there 
through Class XII. He later pursued B.Com from Delhi University and began Company Secretary studies 
while living at an ISKCON Youth Forum BASE in Laxmi Nagar, Delhi.
A major phase of his service involved youth outreach and the organization of increasingly large youth 
festivals. After the COVID period, his responsibilities expanded into institutional administration. He states 
that around 2021, under the direction of H.H. Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj, he took responsibility for 
finance at ISKCON Delhi and for the Bhishma donor-care department. He also describes serving in 
project-finance and coordination responsibilities for the Sri Krishna-Arjuna Temple project at ISKCON 
Kurukshetra.
Central theme: service over status, stewardship over ownership, and long-term relationship over 
one-time transaction.
2. Personal & Family Background
2.1 Birth and Family
Abhay Gaur Prabhuji states that he was born in 1994 in Khatauli, Muzaffarnagar district, Uttar Pradesh. 
He describes himself as the youngest in a family consisting of his parents, one brother and two elder 
sisters.
2.2 Devotional Household
His childhood environment was deeply devotional. The family maintained Radha-Madhava deities at 
home, offered food before eating, organized Bhagavata-katha, visited sacred places and regularly served 
visiting sadhus and saints.
He recalls that when his father built a new house around 1998, a separate room was kept for sadhus and 
saints. As a child, he participated in serving them, including serving prasadam and cleaning their plates. 
In the interview, he identifies this as an important influence in cultivating a service mentality.
3. Spiritual Foundation and ISKCON Connection
3.1 Pilgrimage and Spiritual Culture
The family regularly travelled to holy places, including Badrinath and Vrindavan. Abhay Gaur Prabhuji 
describes pilgrimage, devotional hearing and sadhu association as part of normal family life rather than 
occasional religious activity.

3.2 ISKCON Life Membership and Back to Godhead
According to his account, his father became an ISKCON Vrindavan Life Member around 1999. Thereafter, 
Back to Godhead magazine came regularly to the household and the family's connection with ISKCON 
Vrindavan deepened.
He says the magazine helped him understand spiritual philosophy in accessible language and created 
awareness of festivals such as Ekadashi and Nrisimha Chaturdashi.
Developmental sequence described in the interview: family culture -> sadhu association -> 
pilgrimage -> spiritual literature -> Gurukul -> youth association -> institutional service.
4. Gurukul Education
4.1 Joining Bhaktivedanta Gurukula
He states that he joined Bhaktivedanta Gurukula in Vrindavan in 2003, while in Class III. Before that, he 
had studied at DAV Public School in his hometown. During a family visit to Vrindavan, the family 
encountered information about the Gurukul and eventually chose to enroll him.
4.2 Educational Model
He describes the Gurukul as combining formal academics with spiritual formation and physical 
development.
 ICSE-oriented academic education
 Learning prayers, mantras and shlokas
 Yajna and deity worship
 Application of devotional principles in daily life
 Sports including swimming, basketball and volleyball
 Association with sannyasis, senior devotees and disciples of Srila Prabhupada
He completed his schooling there through Class XII, spending almost a decade in the Gurukul 
environment. He considers these years an extension and strengthening of the devotional foundation 
received at home.
5. Higher Education and ISKCON Youth Forum BASE
5.1 Commerce Education
After Class XII, he entered the commerce field, pursued B.Com from Delhi University and began Company 
Secretary studies.
5.2 Laxmi Nagar BASE
During this period he lived at an ISKCON Youth Forum BASE in Laxmi Nagar, Delhi. He describes the 
BASE environment as an important support structure for maintaining spiritual discipline while pursuing 
education in a metropolitan environment.

5.3 Association as the Core Principle
When explaining BASE and VOICE, he repeatedly returns to one word: association. His view is that when 
young people move away from family for education, their peer group strongly influences their habits, 
discipline and direction.
His recurring youth-development principle: environment and association shape behaviour.
6. Youth Outreach and Major Festivals
6.1 Early Youth Outreach
After moving to Delhi, he became increasingly involved in youth preaching, temple outreach, campus 
programmes and structured student initiatives. The interview also refers to Shreshtha, an initiative for 
Chartered Accountancy students combining academic mentoring, spiritual mentoring and disciplined 
residential life.
6.2 2017 Youth Festival - Approx. 2,000 Participants
In 2017, under the direction of Sundar Gopal Prabhuji, he states that he served as overall coordinator for 
a youth festival at Siri Fort Auditorium, Delhi, targeting approximately 2,000 youth. He recalls being 
deeply inspired by the idea of thousands chanting the Hare Krishna maha-mantra together.
6.3 2018 Expansion - Approx. 5,000 Participants
The following year, the team scaled the programme to approximately 5,000 participants at the Indira 
Gandhi Indoor Stadium complex. The interview mentions participation from university representatives, 
educators, ISKCON leaders and then Union minister Prakash Javadekar.
6.4 Follow-Up as a Core Strategy
He emphasizes that the event itself was not the end goal. Participants were subsequently connected with 
local preaching centres, BASE programmes and continuing spiritual association.
Outreach model: awareness -> participation -> follow-up -> association -> long-term engagement.
6.5 2019 Udgar - Approx. 15,000 Participants
The interview presents the 2019 Udgar festival as a major milestone. A logistical issue roughly 15 days 
before the event led the organizers to shift from a smaller venue to the larger Indira Gandhi Indoor 
Stadium arena. According to the interview, approximately 15,000 youth participated.
The programme included a drug de-addiction theme and, according to the source, was attended by then 
Union Health Minister Dr Harsh Vardhan and several ISKCON leaders. The transcript also states that the 
event entered the World Book of Records. This claim is reported as stated in the source interview and has 
not been independently verified in this report.

7. Transition to Institutional Leadership
After the 2019 youth-festival phase and the disruption caused by COVID-19 in 2020, Abhay Gaur 
Prabhuji's responsibilities shifted toward institutional administration. He states that around 2021 H.H. 
Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj instructed him to take responsibility for temple finances and personally 
trained him in the role.
This transition can be summarized as: youth outreach and event execution -> financial stewardship -> 
donor care -> project coordination.
8. Chief Financial Officer - ISKCON Delhi
8.1 Areas of Responsibility
 Financial transactions
 Financial record keeping
 Financial planning
 Institutional financial sustainability
 Purpose-based utilization of donated funds
8.2 Stewardship Philosophy
His explanation of finance is explicitly spiritual and fiduciary. Money donated for Krishna's service is 
described as Lakshmi belonging to the Lord, and therefore it must be used only for the intended service.
He recalls being trained in a strict standard of financial accountability: even a single paisa should not be 
used outside Krishna's service.
Financial principle: accountability + frugality + purpose-based spending + donor trust.
9. Bhishma Department - Donor Care
9.1 Core Philosophy
Abhay Gaur Prabhuji describes the Bhishma Department as a donor-care department, not merely a 
fundraising function. In his explanation, the relationship should continue after a donation is received: 
the donor should also be spiritually cared for and connected with Krishna and the temple.
9.2 Activities Mentioned
 Donor communication and updates
 Physical and digital mailing
 Festival information
 Ekadashi awareness
 Information about devotional programmes
 Spiritual engagement and relationship building
 Opportunities to support deity worship, temple services and preaching

Donation -> acknowledgement -> communication -> spiritual connection -> relationship building

10. ISKCON Kurukshetra Project
The interview describes his involvement with the Sri Krishna-Arjuna Temple project at ISKCON 
Kurukshetra. The project is presented as connected to a vision attributed to Srila Prabhupada and later 
pursued by H.H. Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj.
Abhay Gaur Prabhuji says he initially supported the project through financial transactions and 
coordination from Delhi. After the departure of Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj, he describes greater 
involvement in continuing the mission and identifies his service at the time of the interview as Project 
Finance Director, contributing to project finance and coordination aimed at accelerating construction.
Because the transcript does not independently establish the present construction status as of September 
2026, this report does not assume that the completion timeline mentioned in the interview remains 
current.
11. Relationship with H.H. Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj
11.1 Administrative Mentorship
Abhay Gaur Prabhuji presents Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj as a central mentor in his development 
as an administrator. He describes Maharaj as a "Prabhupada man" - someone whose life, thinking and 
conduct were deeply dedicated to Srila Prabhupada.
11.2 Frugality and Personal Example
He recounts examples of Maharaj carefully conserving temple resources, including personally switching 
off unnecessary lights and resisting expenditures intended for his own comfort.
One example concerned a proposed lift because climbing stairs had become medically difficult. According 
to Abhay Gaur Prabhuji, Maharaj resisted the proposal for months because he did not want temple 
money spent unnecessarily for personal convenience.
11.3 Final Period of Service
He also states that he had the opportunity to serve Maharaj during his final period in Dehradun and 
during hospitalization. He describes this close association as deeply formative.
Leadership lesson absorbed from his guru: entrusted resources are sacred responsibilities, not 
personal possessions.

12. Leadership & Management Principles
Service before position: Responsibilities are consistently described as seva rather than as status or 
personal achievement.
Mentorship before isolation: Major responsibilities were exercised under guidance from senior leaders.
Think big, scale responsibly: Youth programmes were progressively expanded from roughly 2,000 to 
5,000 to 15,000 participants.
Follow-up before vanity metrics: Event attendance was linked to continuing engagement through BASE 
and preaching centres.
Stewardship before ownership: Institutional finance is treated as entrusted service rather than 
discretionary property.
Relationship before transaction: Donor care continues after the donation through communication and 
spiritual engagement.
Consistency before intensity: Spiritual and behavioural change is linked to regular, sustained practice.
Institutional continuity: He emphasizes carrying forward the mission and processes established by 
senior leaders.
13. Spiritual Guidance Themes
13.1 Consistency in Spiritual Life
When asked how to handle ups and downs in spiritual practice, his main answer is association. He argues 
that people often need support, guidance and the presence of other practitioners to remain regular 
through difficult phases.
13.2 Addiction and the "Higher Taste"
When discussing addiction among youth, he proposes two complementary responses: change association 
and cultivate a higher spiritual taste. He emphasizes chanting, prasadam, positive association and 
regularity.
13.3 Institutional Guidance
When asked about devotees who had hoped to receive initiation from Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj, 
he emphasizes continuing within ISKCON and GBC processes while maintaining respect for Maharaj's 
teachings. This reflects a preference for institutional continuity rather than individualized spiritual 
decision-making outside established structures.

16. Conclusion and Source Note
Abhay Gaur Prabhuji's journey, as presented in the interview, reflects a progression from devotional 
upbringing to disciplined Gurukul education, from education to youth service, from large-scale outreach 
to institutional finance, and from finance to donor care and temple-project coordination.
His family supplied the initial spiritual foundation. Gurukul strengthened discipline and values. ISKCON 
Youth Forum provided a community in which that training could be applied in urban student life. Youth 
outreach developed practical experience in organization, communication, scaling and follow-up. 
Guidance from H.H. Gopal Krishna Goswami Maharaj subsequently shaped his approach to financial 
stewardship and institutional responsibility.
Overall theme: responsible seva - using people, systems, finance and leadership in service of a 
larger spiritual mission.
